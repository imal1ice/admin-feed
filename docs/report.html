Who controls mid-size DeFi? 36 protocols, read from the chain

imal1ice · October 2026 · admin-feed

Between 14 and 26 October 2026 I read the admin setup of 36 DeFi protocols on Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base and Optimism, mostly in the $10M to $400M TVL range. For each one I asked who can upgrade the contracts, mint the token, pause, move collateral or change the oracle, and whether anything slows them down. Everything came from public chain state and verified source code, read with ownerwatch. No protocol is named here: several findings are not fixed yet, and every team was told privately first.

One key, one transaction

Ten of the 36 put at least one privileged role on a single externally owned address. Depending on the protocol, that key can upgrade the contracts, appoint a minter, blacklist holders, approve redemptions or move collateral, in one transaction, with no second signature and no delay. Several of these keys are active hot wallets with hundreds or thousands of transactions. Some teams say the key sits behind MPC or a custody provider. That may be true, but nothing on-chain shows it, and holders cannot check it.

A multisig, but no clock

Sixteen more rely on a Safe with no timelock on at least one admin path. The usual shape: the code upgrade waits 24 to 48 hours as advertised, while the same Safe can change roles, mint limits, oracles or pause state instantly. Thresholds as low as 2-of-3 and 2-of-4 guard tens of millions of dollars.

Supply that did not come through the minter

One stablecoin's documentation says every token is minted one for one against deposited collateral through its minting contract. On-chain, most of the supply ever issued was minted directly by the owner, through a timelock, outside that contract. For two of those mints the timelock's delay was 60 seconds. The tokens may well be backed. The point is that the chain does not show it, and the docs describe a mechanism that most of the supply did not use.

Docs that disagree with the chain

At least ten protocols publish documentation that contradicts their own contracts. The patterns:

  • a timelock described as 30 days that is 3 days on one vault;
  • a "2-day timelock" address that controls nothing, while a different timelock does the work;
  • an address labelled "MultiSig" that is a single key;
  • "immutable" contracts that are upgradeable;
  • a 3-of-4 multisig that is 3-of-5 on-chain;
  • a minting contract listed in the docs that the token no longer uses.

Nowhere to send a security note

Thirteen offered no working private channel. Eleven publish no email at all: Discord, X or Telegram only, often behind phone verification. Two publish a security address that cannot receive mail: one domain has no mail server, and the other address does not exist.

What this means

For users: "audited" and "multisig" on a landing page say little about who can change the protocol tomorrow. The questions that matter can be answered from the chain in minutes: who holds each admin role, how many signatures it takes, and whether anything delays them.

For teams: the fixes are cheap. Put the Safe behind a timelock. Move single-key roles to the Safe. Keep a security.txt with an address that works. Check your docs against the chain whenever ownership changes. A drift check in CI catches the moment docs and chain diverge; the ownerwatch GitHub Action does this for free.

Method and caveats

Read-only public data. Roles were reconstructed from events and confirmed with direct reads. Upgrade authority was resolved from proxy storage, factories and verified upgrade gates, never from owner() alone. One early finding was wrong because an upgrade admin was stored in a proxy factory. It was caught before anything was sent, the tool was fixed in ownerwatch 0.7.6, and every other target was re-checked. The classification is mine. A single key is not proof of bad intent or of a coming exploit. Findings for every team I contacted were re-checked on 26 October 2026; none of the reported keys had been moved yet.

Tools: ownerwatch (MIT, pip install ownerwatch), the ownerwatch GitHub Action, and the live feed of admin changes on Aave, Compound, Lido, Arbitrum DAO and others. Corrections welcome as issues.
